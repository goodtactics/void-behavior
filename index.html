The Void listens.

What it can hear, and how it can answer. A guide for designing the game around it.

It hears three things.

Sound reacts instantly. Words arrive after the visitor stops speaking. Phone events come from the game.

    It answers in light.

    Light is the Void’s language. Only Echo exists today; the rest are proposals to prototype in TouchDesigner.

    Each podium has its own voice.

    Colour, wavelength, speed, viscosity, even a different simulation per podium, blended into one frame.

    Look
    Colour

    Ripple and Pulse drive the wave field; Drift and Glow move light through a second field. In the TouchDesigner build each podium already has its own field, colour and drive frequency. Per-podium speed and viscosity, and Drift and Glow, would be new work.

    All at once.

    Podiums, words and the game overlap. Sound always plays. One mood wins.

      How it decides
      • Sound adds up.Each podium drives its own channel of one field, so hums, knocks and voices overlap and never wait.
      • One mood.Words landing within 250 ms are pooled. The strongest theme wins; ties rotate between podiums.
      • Hold, rest, blend.A mood holds at least 3 s. A podium that was just counted rests 2 s. Moods blend over 1.5 s.
      • Late words expire.A theme still waiting 4 s after the visitor stopped is dropped.
      • Unknown keeps the mood.It fades back to neutral later; 20 s here is a guess.
      • Staff outrank words.Blackout outranks everything, stays until released, and replays nothing.
      • Game events are moments.They play over everything except blackout and never set the mood. Not in the plan yet.

      Cooldown here starts when a podium’s words are counted; the plan doesn’t say yet. Theme strengths and recognition delays are invented.

      Still to decide.

      • Which phone is at which podium?Tap a podium in the app, enter a code shown on it, or match a send button to podium activity.
      • What counts as an answer?Players keep trying until the Void answers. A reply, a fragment, a mood?
      • Which themes?Four to eight, plus neutral. Themes describe the message, never judge the visitor.
      • What is a fragment?A word, a light, a theme. How long it lives, and how the minigame catches it.
      • What does it remember?Sound shapes and themes need no recording. Keeping words for later visitors needs consent.
      • What do the other podiums see?When one theme wins, the rest get nothing yet. A small nod, or silence?
      • Can the game set the mood?Game events only play moments here. A team win could own the mood for a while.
      • Does it wake up over time?An arc across a session or a day could unlock new gestures.
